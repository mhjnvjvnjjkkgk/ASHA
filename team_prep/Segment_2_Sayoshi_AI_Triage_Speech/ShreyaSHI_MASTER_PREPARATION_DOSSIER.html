🚨 Chapter 1: The Core Emergency Problem (The "Why")

What is the "Golden Hour"?

In emergency medicine, the Golden Hour refers to the first 60 minutes following a critical medical emergency—such as an acute STEMI heart attack, ischemic stroke, severe arterial bleeding, or polytrauma. Clinical research shows that if a patient receives definitive medical intervention (such as clot-busting tPA, emergency catheterization, or surgical hemorrhage control) within this 60-minute window, survival rates exceed 85%. After 60 minutes, mortality increases exponentially.

Why Do People Die Today? (The 4 Fatal Flaws of Existing Systems)

In India, ambulances wander an average of 2.4 hospital rejections before finding an open ICU bed. Patients die in transit not because ambulances don't exist, but because of 4 systemic failures:

  1. Static Read-Only Dashboards: Current government bed portals (like West Bengal Swasthya Sathi or Ayushman Bharat) are updated manually once a day by administrative clerks. A dashboard saying "5 beds available" at 10 AM is completely useless at 2 AM when those beds were filled hours ago. Reading a dashboard is not reserving a bed.
  2. The "Ghost Bed" Phenomenon: Hospitals list physical furniture on their websites. But a physical bed frame without an on-duty nurse, intensivist doctor, or oxygen port is a Ghost Bed. If an ambulance arrives at a hospital with 20 physical beds but only 1 nurse on duty, the gate security rejects the patient.
  3. The Multi-Ambulance Race Condition: If a hospital reports 1 open cardiac ICU bed, three different ambulances across the city race toward it simultaneously. The fastest vehicle gets the bed; the other two arrive at the ER gate only to be turned away, having wasted 30 precious minutes.
  4. The En-Route Black Hole (Transit Void): During the 30 to 45 minutes that the ambulance travels to the hospital, medical professionals are absent. Terrified family members panic. They commit fatal mistakes—such as pouring water into an unconscious person's mouth (which enters the lungs and causes instant aspiration asphyxiation and death), or letting a suspected heart attack victim walk (which triples heart oxygen demand and triggers ventricular fibrillation).

Technical Term What It Actually Means Why We Use It in ASHA
WebSocket A permanent, open two-way phone line between the browser and the cloud server. Allows citizen phones and hospital desk tablets to update each other in under 50 milliseconds without reloading the web page.
Supabase An open-source cloud database built on PostgreSQL. Stores all hospital beds, active holds, nurse shifts, and blood stock securely with real-time replication.
Haversine Formula A mathematical equation calculating the straight-line distance between two GPS coordinates on Earth. Lets ASHA filter through 30 Kolkata hospitals in less than 2 milliseconds on low-end smartphones.
OSRM (Open Source Routing Machine) A high-speed navigation algorithm that calculates actual driving paths along real roads. Provides turn-by-turn road polylines and calibrated driving times without paying thousands of dollars for Google Maps API keys.

TTL (Time-To-Live) An automatic countdown timer (e.g. 15 minutes). Ensures that if an ambulance cancels or doesn't show up, the reserved bed automatically unlocks and returns to the public pool.
Soft-Lock A temporary 15-minute hold on a hospital bed while the patient travels. Prevents other ambulances from stealing the bed while you are driving to the hospital.
Hard-Lock Permanent admission of the patient into the hospital bed upon entrance verification. Occurs when the ER nurse verifies the 4-digit OTP at the hospital door.
ESI (Emergency Severity Index) A 5-level clinical triage scale used by emergency rooms worldwide (ESI 1 = Red Resuscitation to ESI 5 = Non-Urgent). Categorizes patient symptoms automatically into Red (ICU), Yellow (Specialized Ward), or Green (General Ward).

export function translateBengaliToEnglish(query: string): BengaliTranslationResult {
  const raw = (query || '').trim();
  const text = raw.toLowerCase();
  const hasBengaliScript = /[\u0980-\u09FF]/.test(raw);

  // 1. Strip family prefixes and possessives (dadur, babar, mayer, kakur, etc.)
  const norm = text
    .replace(/\b(dadur|dadu-r|dadu|dida-r|didar|dida|thakurmar|thakurma|babar|baba-r|baba|mayer|ma-er|ma|kakur|kaku|jethur|jethu|mashir|mashi|pishir|pishi|amar|amader|or|onar|rogi-r|rogir|rogi|patient|patient-er)\b/g, '')
    .replace(/\b(bukh\s*e|buk\s*e|bukhe|buke|buker|bukh|buk|bhook|bhookh|bhuk|bhukh|bhukke|book\s*e|book\s*a|booke|boke|boka|buka|chhati|chhatir|chhatite)\b/g, 'chest')

    .replace(/\b(nite\s*parchena|nite\s*parche\s*na|nite\s*parchina|nite\s*parchi\s*na|nite\s*kosto|nite\s*koshto|shash\s*nite|dam\s*bondho|dom\s*bondho|haphani|hapaani)\b/g, 'distress')
    .replace(/\b(behosh|behoshi|agyan|oggan|gyan\s*nei|sara\s*dichena|sara\s*dichhena|pulse\s*nei|nari\s*cholchena)\b/g, 'unresponsive')

      return {
        translatedEnglish: 'acute crushing chest pain severe shortness of breath respiratory distress heart attack stemi buke betha shash koshto',

export function normalizeBengaliPhonetics(query: string): string {
  // First runs translateBengaliToEnglish(query) to leverage unified dictionaries
  const translation = translateBengaliToEnglish(query);
  let text = translation.translatedEnglish.toLowerCase().trim();

  // Multi-word phrase normalizations for Speech-to-Text misheard dictations (e.g. "book a betha", "bhukke betha")
  text = text
    .replace(/\bbook\s+a\s+betha\b/g, 'buke betha')
    .replace(/\bbhukke\s+betha\b/g, 'buke betha')
    .replace(/\bbhuk\s+betha\b/g, 'buke betha')
    .replace(/\bbooke\s+betha\b/g, 'buke betha')
    .replace(/\bbyatha\b/g, 'betha')
    .replace(/\bkorchhe\b/g, 'korche');

  // Combined fallback guard: Any chest word + pain word = 'buke betha'
  if (text.includes('buke') && (text.includes('betha') || text.includes('pain') || text.includes('chap'))) {
    if (!text.includes('buke betha')) text += ' buke betha';
  }

  return text;
}

Q1. Liability & Malpractice: "What if your triage classifier misinterprets an aortic dissection as acidity, and the patient dies in transit? Who is legally responsible—your team, the hospital, or the app?"

Raw, Airtight Answer:

  • ASHA does NOT issue medical diagnoses or treatment prescriptions. It performs Emergency Severity Index (ESI) Triage Level Allocation (ESI 1–3), which is an administrative prioritization and sorting tool used by 911/108 ambulance dispatchers worldwide.
  • Legal Safe-Harbor Layer: The platform operates under Good Samaritan Immunity Principles (Section 134A of the Motor Vehicles Amendment Act in India).
  • Fail-Safe Fallback: Any ambiguous, unclassified, or borderline query is automatically forced to General Emergency Triage (general_oxygen), ensuring the patient is routed to the nearest operational ER for physical bedside evaluation by a licensed emergency physician.

Q2. Black-Market OTP Scalping & Hoarding: "What if rogue ambulance drivers or touts use automated scripts to soft-lock all ICU beds in the city and demand ₹5,000 cash from desperate families to hand over the OTP?"

Raw, Airtight Answer: ASHA prevents OTP scalping through 4 Hard Cryptographic & Telemetry Rules:

  1. GPS Vector Telemetry Binding: The 4-digit OTP is bound to the physical GPS telemetry stream of the booking smartphone. The Admin Terminal verifies that the device's live GPS telemetry is within 100 meters of the ER gate before allowing OTP redemption. An OTP forwarded to a device miles away is immediately invalidated.
  2. 50% Public Soft-Hold Cap: Hospitals only expose 50% of available staffed beds to public mobile soft-holds. The remaining 50% are reserved exclusively for government 108 ambulance dispatchers and walk-in ER trauma cases.
  3. 1-Hold Per Hardware Device Limit: Strict device-fingerprint rate limiting restricts 1 smartphone to 1 active hold. Automated script attempts trigger immediate hardware-level banning.
  4. Auto-Expiry with Directional Cancellation: If a user moves away (AWAY vector) or stays stationary for >15>15>15 minutes, the hold auto-cancels and the bed is instantly returned to the public pool.

Q3. Ethical Triage Conflict: "A patient with a soft-locked General Ward bed is 2 minutes away. Suddenly, a 108 ambulance carrying an unconscious cardiac arrest patient arrives. Who gets the bed?"

Raw, Airtight Answer:

  • Clinical Priority Preemption Rules: In emergency medicine, resuscitation takes precedence over reservation. An ESI-1 (Red Code Blue) cardiac arrest patient always preempts lower-acuity soft-holds.
  • Automated Re-Routing System: If an ER nurse releases a soft-hold for an incoming Code Blue patient, ASHA's backend instantly sends a high-priority push alert & audio siren to the en-route patient's phone: "Emergency Re-Route: Hospital Resuscitation Bay Full. Rerouting to Backup Facility (2.1 km away, 4 mins)." The app automatically re-engages OSRM navigation to the backup hospital without losing travel time.

Q4. Offline Crisis & Network Blackouts: "During a major disaster (cyclone, flood, cellular tower collapse), cell networks go down completely. How does your app work with 0 internet?"

Raw, Airtight Answer:

  1. Offline-First P2P Mesh Fallback: The app caches all 30 baseline hospital locations, emergency phone numbers, and first-aid protocols locally using Browser IndexedDB & Service Worker Cache.
  2. Direct Cellular Telephony Trigger: When data networks (4G/5G) fail, the app automatically switches to SMS-based fallback routing and direct voice telephone links (tel:108 and tel:{hospital_er}).
  3. Local ER Station Persistence: Hospital Admin Desks store check-in logs locally in IndexedDB on their tablets, syncing back to Supabase automatically once internet connectivity is restored.

Q11. What is the main purpose of ASHA?

Answer: It uses the Haversine formula for fast straight-line distance checks across 30 hospitals, and the OSRM Routing API for actual driving distances and turn-by-turn road paths.

Q13. What is a "Ghost Bed"?

Answer: A ghost bed is a physical bed frame displayed on a dashboard that cannot actually accept a patient because there are no nurses or oxygen points available to support it.

Q14. How does ASHA calculate Staffed Beds?

Answer: Staffed Beds=min⁡(Physical Beds,On-Duty Nurses×Nurse Ratio)\text{Staffed Beds} = \min(\text{Physical Beds}, \text{On-Duty Nurses} \times \text{Nurse Ratio})Staffed Beds=min(Physical Beds,On-Duty Nurses×Nurse Ratio). Nurse ratios are 1:1 for ICU, 1:2 for PICU, and 1:6 for General Wards.

Q15. How long does a bed soft-hold last?

Answer: Exactly 15 minutes (900 seconds).

Q16. How is a soft-hold converted into a permanent admission?

Answer: The patient or paramedic states their 4-digit OTP to the hospital reception nurse, who enters it into the Admin Desk terminal to admit the patient and hard-lock the bed.

Q17. What happens if a patient moves in the wrong direction while holding a bed?
